Some believe chess is a game, war, boring, complicated. I say, chess is a lifestyle; a way to live life. I was introduced to chess when I was around seven or eight years old by my uncle. It would take over 20 years until chess would come back into my life and have a profound effect.

In the summer of 2018 I randomly decided to start playing chess again. I still remembered how to move the pieces and basic strategy — don’t needlessly lose pieces and attack the opponent’s King. My addictive nature instantly kicked in after winning a few games back to back. I put in close to 10,000 games by the end of the year online and over-the-board. During that time I absorbed all that I could about the people I was playing around the world, around Houston, past and current players.

## Wander, Wonder, Engage

After writing [my farewell](https://link.medium.com/Rhuv1DKlAW) to Texas Direct Auto/Vroom and spending time with my buddy John Rodkey at [NowIMS](https://cloud.google.com/now-ims/) I began to search for more people that were adding real value to life.

At the beginning of 2019 I started wandering around Houston and stumbled into [TXRX Labs](https://txrxlabs.org) one day to see what was going on around there. I’ve written a lot of software and wired up hardware but had never made anything physical from raw materials. My first week there I hung out on the couches and talked to whomever would listen. The Lab has all kinds of machines capable of making literally anything you can think of, but it was the stories of the employees, members, volunteers, apprentices, and students that drove me to go back each day.

> It was when I met an instructor and an apprentice in the machine shop that I really began to engage.

### Living on a Chessboard

> Chess holds its matter in its own bonds, shackling the mind and brain so that the inner freedom of the very strongest must suffer. — Albert Einstein

It was probably the first time I walked into the machine shop that I instantly asked, “Can y’all make me a chess set out of metal?!”. That’s when I met the two guys that would go on the two month journey with me to make a set of chess pieces and a board.

The apprentice had made some wrong choices when he was younger and was looking to do better for his daughter. The instructor was looking to make his mark on his own while his dad runs and owns a machine shop. They were two completely different people that had up until this point lived completely different lives. And it was chess and the TXRX community that united us.

### Move, Learn, Move…Repeat

> There’s no other way to find out whether or not you will be successful other than just doing it.— Richard Branson

While playing chess you have a three choices on how to proceed each turn; move a piece, resign, offer a draw.

At each moment of my life I usually move towards a goal, quit, find a compromise. You do not have the choice to stand still; in chess or in life. What if you don’t know how the pieces move?


Our combined skill set was not enough to make this chess set but we had to make a move.

I purchased the materials and gave them the dimensions and standard design. They hit the ground running creating the pieces in CAD so the pieces could be machined. After we had a pawn design we all agreed we had to make it as soon as possible.

![](/images/blog/creating-aluminum-chessmen/02-1_I_RN0msBCgKcf7uDMKCVKg.jpeg)
*Our first pawn made out of steel — we loved it and I still carry it around today*

We began making aluminum pawns and it took five tries before the final machined pawn was perfect. At this point we thought we had all the skills required to complete the entire set!

### Blunder

> Happiness is not the absence of problems, it is the ability to deal with them.— Unknown

We initially estimated that one set would only require six feet of aluminum round stock; now we were on our our second six feet piece of stock.

We had been so enamored with the success of the pawns; as we moved from making pawns to bishops to queens to kings the mistakes began to pile up. We had completely forgotten the most powerful tool at TXRX…the community.

## The Endgame

After being pulled out of our insanity loop by a few TXRX community members progress came very quick.

Ironically it will probably be a while before I actually get my set; one set has been sold already and the team has six more orders to fill.

During this process we all learned way more than we had expected. Especially the apprentice, who was able to get a job as a machinist using chess pieces as proof of his competency and now has his own place to live and enjoy time with his young daughter.

I have always been the one to jump into the fire and solve problems. My time so far interacting with the TXRX community gave me the clarity to understand I am right where I should be. Not only doing what I want to do but doing what I need to do. I learned a lot about CAD, CAM, and CNC.

Next on my agenda is solving some interesting problems I found in the world of manufacturing while continuing to add real value to the Houston communities. So be on the lookout for my next venture…

> Misfits Manufacturing — Building reststops along the road less traveled to support and guide those bold enough to walk the road.
