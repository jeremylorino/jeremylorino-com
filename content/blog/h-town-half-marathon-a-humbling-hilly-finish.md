This morning, I crossed the finish line of my second half marathon—the H-Town Half—with a time of 2:37:36.

It was tough. Much harder than I expected.

But what made the finish special was seeing my mom, my wife, and our two kids waiting for me at the end. That moment reminded me why I started doing this in the first place. Anything can be done if we do it as a family.

Around mile 11, I realized my mouth was bone dry. I hadn’t hydrated well enough before or during the race, and I should’ve brought more water with me. I also needed my last GU earlier—somewhere before mile 12—but I waited too long. That, combined with a surprisingly hilly course, drained me. I hadn’t trained for those inclines, and they made a real impact. If I’m serious about one day running the Houston Chevron Marathon, I’m going to need to start incorporating hill work into my training.

After the run, I was feeling a little down about my time. This race was a full 10 minutes slower than my last half marathon. I caught myself doing what I always say I won’t—comparing my pace to others, as if I’m out here to win. My wife helped bring me back to reality: the course was hard. And it’s okay to have hard races.

This journey isn’t about placing or PRs. It’s about showing up. It’s about showing my kids what hard work and healthy habits look like in real life. That’s the mission. That’s the mindset I want to carry forward.

Now it’s time to recover, reset, and get back to training. The next one is coming up on April 27th.

Let’s go.
