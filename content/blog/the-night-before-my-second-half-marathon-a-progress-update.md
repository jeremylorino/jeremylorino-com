Since crossing the finish line of my first half marathon in February, a lot has changed—and I’ve learned even more.

Shortly after that race, I started feeling some discomfort in my ankle. A visit to the podiatrist confirmed it was Peroneal Tendonitis. That diagnosis set me back three weeks of training, but it also kickstarted a journey into deeper awareness of my body’s needs. I started physical therapy, added daily stretching, and began using resistance bands to strengthen my feet. Now, I’ve built a mini post-workout routine that includes pushups, situps, planks, and foot-strengthening band work. It’s made a difference.

One key change my podiatrist recommended: stop wearing my running shoes full-time. Turns out, running shoes are *only* designed for forward motion—not for everyday use, where lateral support matters a lot more. I’ve since picked up a pair of CrossFit shoes for daily wear, and I reserve my running shoes exclusively for runs. The more you know!

One of the biggest surprises came from my physical therapist’s advice on nutrition. I used to think protein shakes were just for bodybuilders. Turns out, protein and amino acids are essential for *any* kind of muscle recovery—whether you're bulking up or just rebuilding what a long run breaks down. I’ve become consistent about fueling up properly after workouts, and I can feel the difference.

Speaking of fueling: the digestion side of things is still a work in progress. I’ve been taking fiber supplements for about a year trying to get more regular, but I haven’t quite nailed the timing. I still sometimes need a restroom break mid-run, which I’ll factor into tomorrow’s pacing plan.

Last time, I went out too fast—starting at a 9:30/mile pace and running out of steam, finishing with an average pace of 11:00/mile. This time, I’m aiming to stay steady around 10:30/mile, with an overall goal of sticking close to 11:00/mile even if a bathroom stop is necessary. Better pacing = stronger finish.

And the best part? My cheer squad—my mom, my wife, and our two kids—will be at the finish line tomorrow. That alone is enough to carry me through the hard miles.

Here’s to stronger strides, smarter pacing, and lessons learned.
