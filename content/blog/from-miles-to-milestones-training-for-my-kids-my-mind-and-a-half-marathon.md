Running has always been a part of my life, but this journey isn’t just about me. It’s about my kids—and it’s about my mental health. Preparing for a half marathon has become more than a personal challenge; it’s a way to take care of myself both physically and emotionally. Running has helped me manage my depression and anxiety, giving me clarity and focus when life feels overwhelming. Every mile I run and every recovery day I take is part of staying strong, healthy, and present for my family and myself.

Here’s how I’ve been training for February 8th, what I’ve learned along the way, and how my family and running have shaped every step of this journey.

## Starting with a Purpose

My motivation for running has always been rooted in family and self-care. When I lace up my shoes, I’m thinking about my sons and the example I want to set for them. Training for a half marathon has been a chance to show them that dedication and consistency can help you achieve big goals.

This journey truly started back in September, when I decided to take my running seriously and invested in my first real pair of running shoes. They were the most expensive shoes I’ve ever bought, and at the time, I hesitated to spend that much on myself. But from the very first run, I knew they were worth it. These shoes have carried me through countless miles and are a constant reminder of the commitment I’ve made—to myself and to my kids.

Running has also become my way of managing stress and anxiety. Each run feels like a reset, a chance to quiet the noise in my mind and focus on the steady rhythm of my breath and footsteps. It’s not just about the physical health benefits; it’s about finding peace and balance in a busy, chaotic world.

To guide my progress, I put together a plan with four key elements:

1. **Easy Runs:** To build endurance.
2. **Long Runs:** To prepare for race day distance.
3. **Speed Workouts:** To improve my pace and strength.
4. **Recovery Days:** To stay injury-free and enjoy time with my family.

## Building Strength Together

My training days begin long before most people are awake. I get up early, eat breakfast, take my oldest to school, and then hit the pavement for my run. Afterward, I shower and head to work, starting the rest of my day feeling accomplished.

While my sons don’t join me on these runs, they’re still a big part of my motivation. When I come home and share stories about how my training is going, they’re excited to hear about it. They’ve started asking questions about running, and their curiosity reminds me that they’re watching and learning from everything I do. By prioritizing my health and discipline, I hope to show them the importance of setting goals and working steadily toward them.

## Tackling Long Runs With My Why in Mind

Long runs are where I’ve felt the most progress, both physically and mentally. Each week, I’ve pushed my mileage a little further, and some highlights include:

**Dec 21:** A 12-mile run, which reminded me how far I’ve come since starting this journey.

**Dec 28:** A solid 10.5 miles, showing that consistency pays off.

When the miles get tough, I remind myself why I’m out there. I want to be the dad who has the energy to play, the endurance to keep up, and the mindset to push through challenges. Running long distances has also become my way of working through difficult emotions. Those miles are where I process worries and come out feeling lighter.

## Finding Speed for Life’s Challenges

Speed workouts are the hardest part of my plan, but they’ve shown me the value of pushing limits. I’ve incorporated interval sprints and tempo runs, alternating between bursts of effort and periods of rest. These workouts aren’t just about improving my race pace—they’re about building resilience.

In life, as in running, there are times you need to dig deep. When my sons face their own challenges someday, I want them to know it’s okay to struggle as long as you keep moving forward. Running has shown me that even when things feel impossible, you can always take one more step.

## Recovery: Where the Real Connection Happens

Recovery days are as intentional as my training runs, but they’re far from inactive. I spend them walking with my sons, playing games in the yard, or just being present for whatever adventure they dream up. These moments aren’t just about giving my body a break—they’re about investing in my relationship with my boys.

Recovery is also when I feel the full emotional benefits of running. It’s when I can look back at a tough workout or a long run and realize how much stronger I’ve become—physically and mentally. Running has taught me to embrace rest as a part of growth, and that lesson extends far beyond training.

## Lessons From the Road

As I reflect on this journey, it’s clear that running is teaching me just as much about mental health and parenting as it is about endurance.

1. **Consistency Is Key:** Whether it’s showing up for a run or showing up for my family, small daily efforts add up.
2. **Pace Yourself:** Just as I’ve learned to take long runs slow and steady, I’ve realized that life and parenting are marathons, not sprints.
3. **Recovery Matters:** Taking time to recharge—physically and emotionally—makes me better in every role I play.
4. **Movement Helps the Mind:** When my anxiety feels overwhelming or my mood dips, running reminds me that progress is always possible, one step at a time.

## Looking Ahead to Race Day

With six weeks to go, I’m shifting into the final phase of training. I’ll keep building mileage for a few more weeks before tapering down to ensure I’m rested and ready. My sons have already started talking about cheering me on at the finish line, and knowing they’ll be there is all the motivation I need.

On race day, my goal isn’t just to cross the finish line—it’s to show my sons that hard work and dedication pay off. It’s to honor the miles I’ve run to stay strong for them and for myself. Whether I set a personal best or simply soak in the moment, I’ll know I gave it my all.

### Follow My Journey:

I’ll be sharing updates as I get closer to race day, including reflections on training, lessons learned, and how my family and running continue to inspire me. If you’re considering a similar challenge or just looking for motivation, I hope my story encourages you to take the first step—both for yourself and for the people you love most.
