On February 8th, I crossed the finish line of my first half marathon with a total time of **2:28:13** and an **11:18 average pace**, placing **479 out of 702 runners**. It was more difficult than I thought it would be, but it was also one of the most rewarding experiences I’ve had.

This race wasn’t just about the miles — it was about pushing my limits, learning from my mistakes, and figuring out what it will take to go even farther. As I look ahead to running **26.2 miles** in the future, here’s what I took away from my half marathon experience.

## 1. Don’t Change Your Shoes Mid-Training

About midway through training, I experimented with a new pair of shoes that had a **higher arch and a narrower fit**. Bad idea. The change put strain on my ankle, and even though I switched back to my original shoes for race day, my ankle still took a beating.

The lesson? **Stick with what works.** Race day is not the time for experiments. Now, I have to wait for my ankle to recover before I can start training again, which is a setback I could have avoided.

## 2. I Tapered Too Much

Going into the race, I followed the standard tapering approach, gradually reducing my mileage. I **peaked at 12.4 miles** and then started cutting back. But after race day, I realized I might have **tapered too aggressively**.

If I had kept my long runs at **12 miles on Saturdays** while maintaining **7–8 mile weekday runs**, I think I would have had a **stronger and more enjoyable race**. Instead, I felt like I had lost some of the endurance I had built up. Next time, I’ll experiment with a taper that keeps my legs more accustomed to race-day intensity.

## 3. Know Your Limits (and Adjust Expectations Accordingly)

Before the race, I had a **bold goal**: finishing in **2 hours**. I decided to follow the **2:10 pacer** for the first six miles, thinking I could push beyond that pace in the second half.

Reality check.

By mile 6, I realized I **couldn’t sustain that pace** without burning out. I had to **back off** and settle into something more comfortable, or I wouldn’t have finished strong. While it was tough to let go of my original goal, it was the right call.

Going forward, I’ll set **ambitious but realistic** goals, based on my training and actual race-day conditions. Pacing isn’t about ego — it’s about strategy.

## 4. Plan, Plan, and Plan Some More

I thought I had a solid race strategy. I mapped out my fuel plan — **taking GU at miles 0, 3, 6, and 9** — but by mile **12, I was completely out of fuel**. The last mile felt like a **grueling survival test** instead of a strong finish.

I also didn’t pay **enough attention to the course map**. During the race, I found myself **wondering when the next mile marker or porta-potty would appear**, which created unnecessary mental stress.

The takeaway? **Study the course more thoroughly** and refine my fueling strategy so that I don’t run on empty in the final stretch.

## 5. There’s Nothing Like In-Person Support

Having my family cheer me on virtually was great, but nothing compared to seeing **my mom in person at mile 12**.

When I rounded that last mile and saw her standing there, I felt an instant boost of energy. **Serotonin hit. Adrenaline rush.** Seeing her gave me that **final push to finish strong**.

Next time, I’ll make sure more of my family is **physically present** at the race. It makes a world of difference.

## 6. Balancing Training and Family Life

As I tapered leading up to the race, I found myself **playing less with my kids** because I didn’t want to expend extra energy. It was a trade-off, but looking back, I think I could have **trained differently to maintain that balance better**.

Had I kept **higher intensity workouts** instead of fully tapering, I probably would have felt **more confident in using extra energy** for play. Going forward, I’ll tweak my training so I don’t have to **scale back on being present and active with my kids**.

## Looking Ahead: Preparing for 26.2

Now that I’ve conquered **13.1 miles**, I know what needs to change before I take on **26.2**. This half marathon exposed areas for improvement, but it also reinforced why I’m doing this in the first place.

I run for my health.I run to set an example for my kids.I run to push myself beyond what I thought I could do.

While I recover from my ankle injury, I’ll be refining my strategy, learning from these lessons, and getting ready for the next challenge.

**Thanks to everyone who cheered me on, and a huge shoutout to my mom for showing up in person — it meant the world.**

Here’s to the next finish line.
