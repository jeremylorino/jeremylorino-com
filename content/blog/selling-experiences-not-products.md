I have always categorized the company I worked for, Texas Direct Auto — now a Vroom company, as a software company that happens to sell vehicles. But the more I lived it and wrote code for it, I realized that we were not in the software business nor the automotive sales business. We were selling experiences.

### The Product

For most, purchasing a vehicle is an emotional experience. We end up spending a great deal of time and money on our vehicles. For some it’s their baby. For some it’s their lively hood. For some it is a symbol of their hard work.

You work long enough at an online automotive sales company you begin to realize the purchasing of a vehicle is an emotional experience. When purchasing you ask yourself — “Can I afford it?” — “Will I enjoy it?” — “Will it be reliable?” — “Am I getting a good deal?”.

A company in the used automotive industry inherits a lot of doubt; forged throughout history. Traditionally, the lack of transparency by past businesses and limited knowledge available to consumers has led to a stressful interaction between those buying and those selling vehicles.

### The Experience

I have had the awesome opportunity to witness these interactions from the sidelines as a bystander. Most customers pay no attention to me and, the larger we grew as a company, the same has applied to the employees. I was that guy in plain clothes smoking a cigarette and drinking a Redbull just “wondering how your day has been”.


These interactions with customers over almost *nine years* has brought me the realization that it wasn’t just about selling vehicles; it was about selling experiences.

From the dude who’s family just bought him his first car. To the working mother that wants the badass sport SUV that is both practical and fun to drive. And of course the guy who just wanted a dependable truck for work but sadly was sold something less than dependable.

When I was littler I remember hearing about these Disney Imagineers. This team was not only responsible for inventing the impossible but to see it through til the end; when the impossible became the real experience. They don’t just sit in a room and dictate this. They are in the trenches creating the experience. They don’t say “no”; they say “how”.

Looking back on this memory I realize I am extremely lucky to have actually become an “imagineer”.

### The Next Chapter

Now the *Vroom / Texas Direct Auto* chapter in my life has reached its finale. This chapter was full of compounding personal growth; with all those I worked with over the years and those most recently.

I look forward to watching from the sidelines as the Vroom team takes this vision to the moon.

Best of luck y’all!
